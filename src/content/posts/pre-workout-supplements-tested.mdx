The pre-workout supplement category is one of the most cluttered in sports nutrition. Hundreds of products promise "explosive energy," "extreme pumps," and "next-level focus" — most contain similar ingredient profiles with varying dose accuracy. The reality is that 3-4 ingredients have solid research support, and most other listed ingredients are filler. Understanding which ingredients matter helps separate effective products from marketing-heavy alternatives.

This article identifies the evidence-supported ingredients (caffeine, beta-alanine, citrulline), explains why proprietary blends are problematic, and recommends specific products with transparent dosing. The conclusion is that pre-workout supplements can provide meaningful exercise improvements when the right ingredients are at effective doses — but choosing well requires reading past the marketing.

## The three actually-works ingredients


<figure style="margin: 1.5rem 0;">
<img src="/images/illustrations/bwl-c3-preworkout-mix.png" alt="Person mixing pre-workout in shaker bottle before exercise" style="width: 100%; border-radius: 8px; aspect-ratio: 16/9; object-fit: cover;" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

**Caffeine (200-400mg)**:
- Most-researched performance enhancer
- Improves endurance, strength, perceived exertion
- 30-45 minute peak effect
- Best for: most exercise types, especially endurance and high-intensity

**Beta-alanine (3-6g daily, cumulative)**:
- Increases muscle carnosine stores over weeks
- Improves muscular endurance (60-240 second efforts)
- Requires consistent daily use (4-8 weeks to peak)
- Best for: high-rep weightlifting, HIIT, short-distance endurance

**Citrulline malate (6-8g)**:
- Increases nitric oxide production
- Improves muscle pump and possibly performance
- Modest effects on resistance training performance
- Best for: bodybuilding-style training, pump-focused goals

**Honorable mention**:

**Creatine (3-5g daily)** — works but accumulates over weeks; doesn't need pre-workout timing.

Other commonly-listed ingredients (taurine, tyrosine, BCAAs, agmatine, choline, electrolytes) have weak or no research support for performance enhancement at typical pre-workout doses.

## The proprietary blend problem


<figure style="margin: 1.5rem 0;">
<img src="/images/illustrations/bwl-c3-preworkout-focus.png" alt="Athlete with eyes closed before workout in home gym" style="width: 100%; border-radius: 8px; aspect-ratio: 16/9; object-fit: cover;" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

"Proprietary blends" hide individual ingredient doses behind a combined total. Example:

**Brand A "Energy Matrix" 4.5g**:
- Beta-alanine
- Caffeine
- Citrulline malate
- Taurine
- L-tyrosine

Without individual doses, you don't know:
- Is beta-alanine at the effective 3g dose, or just 100mg trace?
- Is citrulline at the 6g effective dose, or just 200mg?
- How much caffeine is hidden in there?

Typically, proprietary blends contain effective doses of the cheap ingredient (caffeine) and trace amounts of expensive ingredients (citrulline). The marketing implies all ingredients are doing work; the reality is most are insufficient.

**Transparent labeling** (what to look for):
- Each ingredient listed with individual mg dose
- Effective doses for primary ingredients (caffeine 200-300mg, beta-alanine 3-4g, citrulline 6-8g)
- Brands using this: Legion, Transparent Labs, Naked Nutrition, Bulksupplements

Avoid proprietary blends. The "secret formula" marketing is a red flag for inadequate dosing.

## Caffeine dosing and tolerance


<figure style="margin: 1.5rem 0;">
<img src="/images/illustrations/bwl-c3-preworkout-scoop.png" alt="Single scoop of pre-workout powder beside glass of water" style="width: 100%; border-radius: 8px; aspect-ratio: 16/9; object-fit: cover;" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

Caffeine is the most-effective pre-workout ingredient but also the most user-dependent:

**Standard adult dose**: 3-6mg per kg body weight
- 70kg adult: 210-420mg
- 90kg adult: 270-540mg

**FDA safe daily maximum**: 400mg total caffeine from all sources

**Tolerance development**:
- Daily 300mg caffeine for 2-4 weeks → reduced effects
- Caffeine effectiveness restored after 5-7 day break

**Strategic use**:
- 3-4 sessions per week (alternating stim-free days)
- Maintains caffeine effectiveness
- Sleep-protective if used before 2 PM

**Caffeine sensitivity warning signs**:
- Jitters, anxiety, racing heart at 200mg+
- Sleep disruption from afternoon caffeine
- Headache when missing caffeine ("withdrawal")

For caffeine-sensitive users, start at 100mg pre-workout and increase gradually. For tolerant users, 200-300mg per session is the sweet spot.

## Beta-alanine tingling


<figure style="margin: 1.5rem 0;">
<img src="/images/illustrations/bwl-c3-preworkout-lift.png" alt="Athlete lifting weights with focused intensity in home gym" style="width: 100%; border-radius: 8px; aspect-ratio: 16/9; object-fit: cover;" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />
</figure>

Beta-alanine produces a characteristic tingling sensation ("paresthesia") in 30-50% of users at standard 3-4g doses. The tingling:

- Occurs in skin of face, neck, arms
- Starts 10-20 minutes after dose
- Lasts 30-60 minutes
- Subsides as body adapts (usually within 2-4 weeks)
- Harmless physiologically (intracellular nerve activation, not damage)

**To minimize tingling while maintaining benefit**:
- Split daily dose: 1.5g twice daily instead of 3g once
- Take with food
- Use sustained-release formulations
- Tolerate it — it goes away

**Beta-alanine isn't required for pre-workout**:
- Can be taken any time of day
- Effect is cumulative, not acute
- Often taken as separate supplement rather than in pre-workout blend

## Reasons to skip pre-workout entirely
A pre-workout is optional. Many good sessions need only sleep, food, water, a warm-up, and a plan. Skip stimulant products if you train late, are caffeine-sensitive, have anxiety or palpitations, use medications that interact with stimulants, are pregnant, have uncontrolled blood pressure, or were told to limit caffeine. A supplement should never be the reason you ignore fatigue, illness, chest symptoms, or poor recovery.

## Non-commercial pre-workout checklist
- Sleep: if sleep debt is high, lower the workout target before adding stimulants.
- Food: a normal carbohydrate-containing meal often solves low-energy training.
- Caffeine: coffee or tea can be easier to dose than a proprietary scoop.
- Warm-up: ten progressive minutes can outperform a supplement for readiness.
- Label: avoid hidden-dose blends, banned-stimulant lookalikes, or multiple caffeine sources.
