  - question: "Do I really need electrolytes during exercise?"
    answer: "For under 60 minutes of moderate exercise, plain water is usually sufficient. Beyond that — and especially for hot weather, heavy sweating, or high-intensity sessions — electrolytes become important. Heavy sweaters lose 500-1500mg sodium per hour. Replacing only water (not sodium) can cause hyponatremia (low blood sodium), which produces symptoms ranging from headache to seizures. Endurance athletes, hot-climate exercisers, and salty sweaters benefit most from electrolyte supplementation."
  - question: "How much sodium do I need during exercise?"
    answer: "ACSM recommends 300-600mg sodium per hour for endurance athletes, more for heavy sweaters in hot conditions. Sweat sodium concentration varies widely (300-2000mg per liter of sweat). Salty sweat (visible white residue on dark clothing, salty taste): need more. Bland sweat: need less. For most exercisers under 60 minutes, no additional sodium needed. For 1-2 hour sessions in normal weather: 200-400mg sodium total. For 2+ hour sessions or hot conditions: 400-800mg per hour."
  - question: "What's the difference between LMNT, Liquid IV, and Gatorade?"
    answer: "LMNT: 1000mg sodium, 200mg potassium, 60mg magnesium per packet, no sugar. Best for keto, low-carb, or pure electrolyte focus. Gatorade: 110-160mg sodium, sugar-based, designed for typical athletic use. Lower mineral content than premium alternatives.   - question: "Can I make my own electrolyte drink?"
    answer: "Yes, easily and cheaply. Total cost: $0.10 per liter vs $1-2 for commercial alternatives. The homemade version works equally well for endurance training.   - question: "When should I worry about electrolyte imbalance?"
    answer: "Symptoms of imbalance: muscle cramps (insufficient sodium or magnesium), fatigue, headache, nausea, dizziness, confusion. Cramps specifically often indicate sodium loss without replacement. Severe hyponatremia (drinking too much water without sodium) is rare but dangerous — primarily in endurance events where athletes drink excessively. The signs of dangerous hyponatremia: nausea + vomiting + confusion + headache after sustained exercise with high water intake."
  - question: "Do non-athletes need electrolyte supplements?"
    answer: "Most non-athletes get sufficient electrolytes from diet. Sodium especially is abundant in typical American diets. Potassium is the more common deficit (most Americans don't hit the 4700mg daily recommendation). Magnesium deficiency is widespread but less acute. For non-athletes, focus on food sources first: bananas, leafy greens, nuts, beans for potassium; whole grains, dark chocolate, leafy greens for magnesium. Electrolyte supplementation has moved from niche endurance athlete product to mainstream wellness category. Brands like LMNT and Liquid IV have built large businesses selling salt-based drink mixes to general consumers. The marketing often overstates the need; the underlying science is sound for specific use cases. Understanding when electrolytes matter (and when they don't) helps optimize spending and avoid both deficits and unnecessary supplementation.

This article explains electrolyte chemistry, identifies the situations where supplementation matters, and compares the major brand approaches. The conclusion is that most active adults benefit from some electrolyte awareness during heavy exercise but rarely need daily supplementation outside of specific sweating conditions.

Plain water is sufficient for:
- Light/moderate exercise under 60 minutes
- Normal indoor or temperate outdoor conditions
- Daily hydration in non-exercising adults
- Drinking with meals (food provides electrolytes)

Electrolytes become important for:
- **Endurance exercise 60+ minutes**: Sodium loss accumulates
- **Hot weather exercise** (above 75°F / 24°C): Increased sweat rate
- **Heavy sweaters**: Some individuals lose 1-2L sweat per hour
- **Salty sweaters**: Visible salt crystals on clothing post-exercise
- **Athletes in calorie deficit**: Reduced food intake = reduced dietary electrolytes
- **Recovery from illness**: Vomiting/diarrhea depletes electrolytes
- **Hot climate workers**: Outdoor occupations in summer

The ACSM recommends 300-600mg sodium per hour during exercise lasting longer than 1 hour, with higher amounts (up to 1000mg/hour) for heavy sweaters or hot conditions.

**Short exercise (under 1 hour)**: 0 additional sodium needed
**Moderate exercise (1-2 hours, normal weather)**: 200-400mg sodium total
**Long exercise (2+ hours, normal weather)**: 400-600mg per hour
**Hot weather (75°F+ / 24°C+)**: 500-800mg per hour
**Heavy sweaters in hot conditions**: 800-1200mg per hour

**Identifying salty sweaters**:
- White salt crystals on dark clothing after exercise
- Salty taste of sweat
- Sweat that stings eyes
- Family history of cramping with prolonged exercise

For most recreational exercisers, daily sodium intake from food (typically 3000-4000mg) covers exercise sodium loss adequately for sessions under 90 minutes. Beyond that, supplementation becomes useful.

**LMNT**:
- 1000mg sodium, 200mg potassium, 60mg magnesium per packet
- No sugar (suits keto, low-carb)
- $1.50-2.50 per packet
- Best for: keto users, heavy sweaters, those wanting pure electrolytes

**Liquid IV**:
- 500mg sodium, 370mg potassium per packet
- Contains glucose for "cellular transport"
- 11g sugar per packet
- $1.50-2 per packet
- Best for: those who want flavor variety and don't mind sugar

**Gatorade Endurance Formula**:
- 300mg sodium, 140mg potassium per bottle
- Sugar-based (35g per bottle)
- $2-3 per bottle
- Best for: traditional sports drink preference

**Skratch Labs**:
- 380mg sodium, 39mg potassium per scoop
- All-natural ingredients
- $1.50-2 per serving
- Best for: clean ingredient focus

**Nuun**:
- 360mg sodium, 100mg potassium per tablet
- Tablet form, low sugar
- $0.65-1 per tablet
- Best for: convenience and travel

For heavy-sweat endurance athletes, LMNT's higher sodium dose is appropriate. For general exercise hydration, Nuun or Skratch provide adequate sodium without excessive amounts. For sugar-tolerant users wanting fast carb + electrolytes, Gatorade or Liquid IV.

</figure>

The cheapest effective option:

**Per 1 liter of water**:
- 1/2 to 1 teaspoon table salt (1200-2400mg sodium)
- 1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon salt substitute / potassium chloride (550-1100mg potassium)
- 1-2 tablespoons sugar OR 2-4 oz fruit juice (carbohydrate for absorption)
- Optional: lemon or lime juice for flavor

Cost: $0.05-0.15 per liter vs $1-2 for commercial alternatives.

The homemade version works equally well biochemically. The carbohydrate isn't strictly necessary but does aid sodium absorption via the sodium-glucose cotransporter mechanism.

For users wanting commercial convenience and flavor variety, the brands above are easier. For users prioritizing cost, homemade is dramatically cheaper.

## The buying decision

For most active adults who exercise 60+ minutes regularly, Nuun Sport tablets at $25-35 hit the sweet spot. The tablet convenience, reasonable sodium dose, and flavor variety cover daily exercise hydration needs.

For heavy sweaters or serious endurance athletes, LMNT at $45-60 provides the higher sodium dose appropriate for hot-weather long sessions. The keto compatibility appeals to specific user populations.

For flavor-focused users without keto restrictions, Liquid IV at $20-30 offers the widest variety of palatable flavors.

For pure cost optimization, homemade electrolyte drinks ($0.05-0.15 per liter) are dramatically cheaper than any commercial product and equally effective.

Avoid drinking pure plain water for hours of exercise in hot conditions. Hyponatremia from over-hydration without electrolyte replacement is rare but dangerous. Sodium replacement matters more than buyers expect for sustained heavy sweating.

Electrolyte awareness is one of the under-discussed fitness optimizations. Many cramping issues, fatigue complaints, and "I'm so tired after exercise" reports trace back to inadequate sodium replacement. For active adults, even moderate electrolyte supplementation during long sessions produces noticeable improvement in exercise tolerance and recovery.
