## Quick decision table

| Situation | Safer choice | Mistake to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Desk discomfort starts | Stand, breathe, check shoulder range, and do one easy mobility drill | Waiting until pain changes your form |
| Microbreak feels helpful | Repeat every 45-60 minutes and keep it gentle | Turning every break into a hard workout |
| Strength work is planned | Use slow band rows, wall slides, and pain-free range | Training through sharp, radiating, or worsening pain |
| Red flags appear | Stop and seek qualified care for numbness, weakness, chest symptoms, trauma, or severe pain | Treating medical warning signs as poor discipline |

![setup visual](/images/illustrations/bwl-shoulder-microbreak-20260625-01.png)

## 1. Read the desk signal before choosing an exercise
A desk worker shoulder plan should begin with what changed today: keyboard reach, meeting length, mouse use, stress,
sleep, and whether the shoulder feels stiff or painful before exercise. Start with gentle motion and posture reset
rather than a loaded drill. If movement improves warmth and range, continue with the lighter plan; if pain sharpens,
spreads, or reduces strength, stop and use qualified medical guidance.

![supporting visual 2](/images/illustrations/bwl-shoulder-microbreak-20260625-02.png)

## 2. Use microbreaks to reduce position fatigue
Microbreaks work best when they are boring and repeatable. Stand up, relax the neck, move the shoulder blades, breathe,
and do one or two low-effort drills that do not create symptoms. The goal is to interrupt long static positions so a
later workout does not start from a clenched, irritated baseline.

![supporting visual 3](/images/illustrations/bwl-shoulder-microbreak-20260625-03.png)

## 3. Progress strength without provoking symptoms
Strength progressions should be small: band rows, wall slides, external-rotation practice, and carrying work that stays
below pain. Avoid grinding reps, sudden overhead loading, or copying rehab exercises meant for someone else. Keep a
simple note of the drill, effort, symptoms during the set, and the next-day result.

![supporting visual 4](/images/illustrations/bwl-shoulder-microbreak-20260625-04.png)

## 4. Set red flags that stop the plan
Red flags matter more than streaks. Stop for numbness, tingling, sudden weakness, trauma, chest symptoms, severe
headache, fever, or shoulder pain that is escalating despite lower load. A practical blog post should make this boundary
obvious rather than implying a home checklist replaces clinical assessment.
